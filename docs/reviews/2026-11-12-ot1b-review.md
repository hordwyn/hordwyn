### OT1B-3 (medium) The local order of the owner's two titles is unchanged; the suite does not assert it

- Where: `crates/search/src/pg.rs:133-141`; `crates/search/tests/suite.rs:72-73`; DEC-607.
- Problem: "Harry Potter and the Order of the Homeless" and "Harry Potter and the
  Philosopher's Stone" both start with "harry potter" and neither is the query, so both get
  `whole = false`, `lead = true` and are ordered by `ts_rank + similarity` exactly as on

  sentence. On Meilisearch the pair ties on `wordPosition` and `exactness` in the same way.
- Failure scenario: the owner repeats his test. TMDB is skipped (no key, rate limit,
  provider down) or slow: the palette shows the kept copies under
  "tmdb - Movie" in our order, derivative first, and it stays that way. Or he uses the REST

- Suggested fix: no cheap code fix exists; this is step 3 of the diagnosis (popularity) and
  the re-check after DATA-0. Until then: (a) say in the comment of `title_ties` that the

  (b) tell the owner in the handoff, in one sentence,
  that the fix for his pair is "TMDB's order in the palette", not our index. One arbitrary
  factor could be removed cheaply: `weighted` indexes `original_title` even when it equals
  `title`, which doubles the rank of exactly those documents (`pg.rs:50-64`).

### OT1B-4 (medium) Postgres: a title with a leading article now ranks below every title that starts with the bare word

- Where: `crates/search/src/pg.rs:134`, `:141`, `:159`.
- Problem: `lead` is a plain `starts_with` on the lower-cased title and is ordered before
  the text score. Query "avengers": "Avengers: Endgame", "Avengers: Infinity War",
  "Avengers: Age of Ultron", "Avengers Grimm" get `lead = true`; "The Avengers" gets
  `false` and now sorts after all of them. On HEAD the score decided, and `similarity(body,
  'avengers')` is highest for the shortest body, "The Avengers". The same for "matrix"
  ("Matrix", the 1993 series, is `whole`; "The Matrix" is neither), "godfather", and the
  German articles ("Der Pate", "Die Tribute von Panem"). This is the owner's complaint
  (a derivative above the original) produced by the fix, on the fallback backend. On
  Meilisearch `wordPosition` already behaved like this before the package, so the two
  backends now agree; that is why this is not a must-fix.
- Failure scenario: instance on the Postgres backend, TMDB not answering, catalog holds the
  kept copies of an earlier "avengers" search: the 2012 film is around row ten.
- Suggested fix: compare against the title without one leading article, for example
  `regexp_replace(lower(fields ->> $6), '^(the|a|an|der|die|das|ein|eine|le|la|les|el|los|las|il) ', '')`
  for both `whole` and `lead` (keep the raw comparison too, so "The Thing" still equals the
  query "the thing"); add "The Avengers" / "Avengers: Endgame" with query "avengers" to
  `title_ties` for Postgres. Decide separately whether Meilisearch should follow (it would
  need a normalised title attribute). Diacritics and case were checked and are no new
  problem: a query without the accent ("amelie") does not reach the `exact` phase at all
  (`simple` configuration, no `unaccent`), so the tie-break never sees it, as before.

### OT1B-10 (low) DEC-607's cost numbers do not cover rows whose `fields` are stored out of line

- Where: `pg.rs:133-134`; DEC-607.
- Problem: the old statement only carried `fields` through the sort; the new one reads
  `fields ->> $6` for every matching row before the cut. The measurement used titles only
  and 600-character descriptions, both stored inline. A `fields` value above the storage
  threshold (about 2 kB after compression) has to be fetched and decompressed per matching
  row. The variant on `body` was measured as "not faster" on the same inline data.
- Failure scenario: a book-heavy catalog with long descriptions and a one-word query that
  matches thousands of rows; the added cost is then no longer the measured 6 to 7 ms. Not
  measured here (no database client outside the dev shell), so the size is unknown.
- Suggested fix: at the next load run seed a third of the documents with 4 kB descriptions
  and repeat the EXPLAIN. If it shows: `lead` can be computed from `body` (it starts with
  the title), `whole` from `body = $3 OR starts_with(body, $3 || ' ')` plus a recheck on
  the 20 returned rows, or a stored lower-cased title column.
- Plan stability, checked: the WHERE clauses are unchanged and the two new expressions sit
  in the target list and the sort, so the choice between custom and generic plan is the
  same as on HEAD; the sixth parameter is not used by any index condition. The forced
  generic plan of the measurement is the slow case for old and new alike (`$3 = ''` cannot
  be folded, so the OR keeps the GIN indexes out); the release load runs (84 to 122 ms p95)
  were made through the application's own prepared statements.

unchanged; the measured worst case is +6 to 7 ms on the two broad one-word queries and
